Where nothing is reported, it adds a clearly labelled balance-sheet proxy. Every figure links to its official source. Somebody is always chasing an invoice. What this is

Large UK companies must publish, twice a year, how long they take to pay invoices. Spanish companies must state their average supplier payment period in their annual accounts. Both are public, and both are awkward to use: the UK data is filed per legal entity, the Spanish data sits inside annual reports. The Chase Index cleans them, links UK entities to their parent groups where that can be done with confidence, and keeps the source link for every number.

Supplying a large UK company? Look up how long your customer takes to pay. It searches {{ h.lookup_n|int_c }} UK legal entities by name or company number.

Reported figures are what companies filed under a legal duty. Computed DPO (proxy) is days payable outstanding, calculated from published financial statements. It is not the same thing as reported days to pay and is always shown separately. More in the methodology and the limitations.
