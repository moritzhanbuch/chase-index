What an agent on both sides would change here
  • 29% of UK invoices are paid after the agreed terms. An agent on the AP side matches invoice, PO and proof of delivery and clears disputes with the supplier's agent before the due date, and the ERP posts only after human approval.
  • 4 UK legal entities report separately. Agents work the long tail of suppliers that AP teams rarely get to, across entities, and email suppliers that are not on the network yet.
  • Long payment days are leverage that can be priced. Buyer and supplier agents negotiate early payment against a discount, inside guardrails the CFO sets (minimum APR, maximum discount, blocklists).

Suggested first conversation

Ask for the head of accounts payable or shared services. Open with the public number: Pernod Ricard's UK entities pay in 63 days on average (weighted by invoice value, 4 entities; UK median 32), and Chivas Brothers Limited is up 10 days to 69 days year on year. With 29% of invoices paid after the agreed terms, start with process: what stops an invoice from being approved on time (a missing PO match, an unconfirmed delivery, a supplier waiting on an answer)? UK entities report offering supply chain finance: ask which suppliers it reaches, and did terms get longer when the programme came in?
