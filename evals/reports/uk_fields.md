## 4. Dispute-share threshold

`dispute_share_high` fires when the group's share of invoices not paid within terms because of a dispute is at or above **10%** (invoice-value-weighted across its current UK entities; simple mean when a value is missing).

- Entity distribution (n=6,158): 70.6% report exactly 0; median 0, p75 1, p90 7, p95 18.15, p99 70.
- 10% sits at percentile 91.7 of current entities and percentile 94.7 of matched groups (n=473).
- Why this number: the distribution is a spike at zero with a long tail, so a percentile near the top decile is the natural cut. The entity p90 is 7%; 10% is the next round number, easy to say in a sentence.
- Groups flagged: 25 of 473.
- Caveat: the field is by number of invoices and self-reported. Some filers enter 0 because they cannot measure it, and a few say so in their dispute text. A 0 is not evidence of no disputes.
- Two readings of the field (found in the Phase 12 QA): 275 current entities report a dispute share ABOVE their own share of invoices paid late, which is only possible if they read the field as 'share of the late invoices that were late because of a dispute'. Those values are left out of every group figure and signal (`dispute_share_inconsistent_n`). Values at or below the late share may still use either reading, so the dispute share is quoted only as the filer's own figure with its label, never converted into an invoice count.

## 5. Dispute-process rubric

Two questions from the supplier's side: where do I go, and what happens next?

| Level | Definition |
|---|---|
| `named_route` | Says where to go (email address, phone, portal, helpdesk, or a named team or role) AND what happens next (a stated timescale, or an escalation step beyond first contact, including mediation, adjudication or arbitration). |
| `basic` | Only one of the two: a contact, or concrete described steps (invoice on hold, credit note requested), but not both. |
| `boilerplate` | Generic statement with neither, e.g. "disputes are resolved in line with contract terms", "discussion with the supplier". |
| `none` | Empty, "n/a", a bare web link, or a text that does not describe a dispute process. |

Reading conventions for the LLM labels: "if AP cannot resolve it, it goes to X" counts as escalation, an unconditional internal hand-off does not; a contract clause with timescales and arbitration but no contact is `basic`; a team or role counts as a route even without an address, "the relevant department" does not.

The rubric rates the disclosure, not the company's behaviour.

## 6. Regex rules vs LLM read

## 8. What is NOT in the data

- No deductions field. Only the two pay-to-stay yes/no fields described in section 1.
- The supply-chain financing flag is a self-declared yes/no. No programme size, provider, uptake, or whether it is reverse factoring or dynamic discounting.
- The e-invoicing flag does not say which standard, portal or network, or what share of invoices arrives electronically.
- No early-payment discount field of any kind.
- The dispute share is by number of invoices, not value, and is null under the oldest rules (Regime-1).
- "Payment terms have changed" is a free-text field filled only by companies that declare a change. An empty field means none was declared, not that none happened.
- No field says how many suppliers or invoices a company has.

## 9. Caveats

- All flags are self-reported by the company and signed off by a director; nobody audits them.
- Reports are per UK legal entity. A group row describes its matched UK entities, not the global group.
- `scf_started` / `einvoicing_started` compare with the report about one year earlier (within 92 days). Flags flip for clerical reasons too; treat a start as a prompt to check the filing, not as a confirmed launch.
- The payment-code flag is true for any text other than "No". A few texts name internal codes or are not codes at all (`payment_code_name` = other_or_unclear). The Prompt Payment Code was replaced by the Fair Payment Code, and older wording persists in filings.
- Direction of a terms change comes from keyword rules on short free text; `other` and `unknown` are common and honest.
- Maximum contractual period contains typos (values far above 365 days); the year-on-year deltas inherit them.
- Dispute texts are public company statements that sometimes name staff. No output column extracts names; the trimmed text is the only place they can appear, and it should stay off any public page unless filtered.
- Blind QA sample: `evals/gold/uk_dispute_sample.csv` (kept (QA labels present)); key in `data/interim/uk_dispute_sample_key.csv`.

### Verdict

- The LLM labels are good enough to drive the weak-disclosure signal: 97% binary agreement (kappa 0.93), no false weak flags in this sample.
- The regex labels are not. On the weak-vs-not split, kappa is 0.39 and recall is 42%: the regex misses more than half of the boilerplate/none texts, almost always by promoting them to `basic` because a link, "contact", "dispute resolution" or "relevant parties" fired a rule. Its precision when it does say weak is 83% (10/12), so a regex `boilerplate`/`none` is usually right, but the signal built on it undercounts weak disclosure by about half and is biased toward companies whose text happens to avoid the trigger words.
- Recommendation: drive the sales signal from the LLM label (regex as a fallback only where no LLM label exists, and say so), or tighten the regex so a bare URL, "contact the supplier/vendor", and contract or negotiation phrases with no team or address do not count as a route. The named_route/basic boundary is subjective (my own disagreements with the LLM sit there), so do not use that boundary for anything customer-facing.
- Open item: check the full text behind eacbe822319d8260 (Kier Services), which is truncated in the sample.
