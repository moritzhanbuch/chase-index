# External validation of the ranking (Phase 12, step 7)

**Verdict: partly validated.** The tiering reproduces on a blind holdout (which reuses the same UK evidence, so it is a consistency check). Tier 1 accounts disclose a supplier finance programme more often: 34% vs 21% (1.6 times the rate; odds ratio 2.0, size-adjusted 1.9). Public vendor case studies show **no** significant link. The evidence that the ranking predicts *buying* is thin. What it does show is that the ranking finds companies that hold suppliers' cash for long periods and already run financing programmes around it. A programme is not the same as demand for an agent network: it can mean terms were extended through a funder. Whether those companies buy an agent network is untested until your team's own pipeline data exists (the feedback loop in `config/status/` is built for that).

All numbers: `evals/reports/external_validation_numbers.md`, regenerated by `.venv/bin/python evals/external_validation.py`. No weight was changed because of these tests.

## 1. Blind holdout gold set

- **What:** 40 companies (20 expected Tier 1, 20 expected Tier 3), picked by an agent that saw no scores, no tiers and not the calibration set. Procedure: shuffled with seed 7, first qualifiers kept, at most 3 per sector per class (`evals/gold/calibration_holdout_README.md`).
- **Result:** 15/20 expected Tier 1 land in Tier 1, 19/20 expected Tier 3 land in Tier 3. Severe misses (expected Tier 1 in Tier 3, or the reverse): 2/40.
- **Caveat, important:** the holdout's "ten" rule uses UK reported days to pay, the same evidence the pain score uses. This tests that the tiering logic generalises beyond the hand-picked calibration set. It does not test whether slow payment predicts buying.
- **What the misses taught us:**
  - *Renault* (expected Tier 3) was Tier 1 because two Nissan UK entities had been rolled into Renault through a stale Wikidata parent link (Renault holds a minority stake). **Fixed** (`config/parent_overrides.yaml`, DECISIONS 37). Renault is now Tier 3.
  - *Parker Hannifin* (expected Tier 1, model Tier 3): its UK entities (Meggitt, acquired 2022) are not linked to the group, so it is scored on computed DPO. Entity-resolution recall gap, open.
  - *Booking Holdings* (expected Tier 3, model Tier 2): UK entities not linked; the DPO proxy is meaningless for an online travel agent (payables to hotels). Recall gap plus a known proxy weakness, open.
  - *Drax* (expected Tier 1, model Tier 3): the holdout used the median entity (43 days), the model weights by invoice value (32 days: most of the value is paid fast). A method difference, and the model's reading is defensible.
  - *Thermo Fisher, Elanco, TransDigm*: Tier 2, one step short. Borderline by design.

## 2. Supplier finance programmes (an outside signal of working-capital focus; not used in any score)

- **What:** whether a company tags a supplier finance obligation in its SEC XBRL filing (ASU 2022-04), from `data/interim/sfa_universe_tags.csv` (all 1,909 universe companies scanned, 190 SEC filers tagged). SEC filers only: ESEF notes are block-tagged, so "not tagged" means nothing there. Tiers are compared **before** the signal upgrade (`base_tier`). Supplier finance adds nothing to any score (DECISIONS 38), so the test is not circular.
- **Result (837 SEC filers):** Tier 1 34% tagged vs 21% for Tier 2 and 3 (1.6 times the rate); odds ratio 2.0, Fisher p = 0.002. Size-adjusted (revenue terciles): odds ratio **1.9**.
- **The clean subset:** where pain comes from **UK statutory days** (a separate source from US XBRL), Tier 1 40% vs 24% (1.7 times the rate), odds ratio 2.1, p = 0.009; size-adjusted **2.2**. This is the strongest external association in the project. It is an association with financing activity, not with buying an agent network.
- **Where it does not hold:** pain from computed DPO: odds ratio 1.3, p = 0.30. A supplier finance programme lengthens payables, so any link there would be partly mechanical anyway. In the smallest revenue third there is no effect.
- **Caveats:** tagging depends on materiality (small programmes are not tagged). A programme can mean the buyer extended terms and moved the cost to suppliers through a funder (Alstom's own note: terms extended by 0 to 20 days), or that it is already served by a bank or Taulia. That is why supplier finance is shown on every surface but adds nothing to any score (DECISIONS 38).

## 3. Named customers of AP automation, early-payment and supply chain finance vendors

- **What:** 129 companies named on public vendor pages (SAP Taulia, C2FO, Basware, Tradeshift, PrimeRevenue, Medius, Kyriba, Serrala, Esker, Tungsten, Orbian, bank programme pages), `data/interim/public_scf_case_studies.csv`, scout report `evals/reports/case_studies_scout.md`. The strict set drops a 2012 gov.uk pledge list (intent, not a programme): 65 matched scored accounts.
- **Result:** Tier 1 7% vs 4%; odds ratio 1.7, p = 0.12; size-adjusted 1.5. Including the 2012 list: 1.6, p = 0.12. On the UK-evidence subset: 1.3, p = 0.34. **Not significant.**
- **Why it is weak evidence either way:** 65 positives give low power; vendors showcase famous logos, not their most typical customers; absence from a vendor page means nothing; several vendors blocked access (Coupa, Demica, Ivalua).

## What this means for your team

- Use the ranking to decide **who to talk to first**. Do not use it as a forecast of who buys.
- For the GTM team, a disclosed programme is a question to ask, not a reason to call: who does it reach, who pays for it, did terms get longer when it started? The long tail a bank programme does not reach is where an agent network differs.
- The real validation is pipeline outcomes. After 8 to 12 weeks of using the queue, `make feedback` compares reply and meeting rates by tier. That is the test that matters, and it needs their data.
